Reel Math

The yardage sticker assumed one diameter of mono. Braid lies by diameter, the rod's window is a loading range, and the quarter-ounce drawer belongs to a lake at dawn.

The spool truth

A spool holds area times length, so capacity scales with the square of the diameter ratio. The 200 yards of 10 lb on the sticker is a different spool in braid - and the spool never read the box.

The casting window

The 3/8 to 3/4 oz printed on the blank is the range that loads it. Under it the blank barely bends; past it the blank folds. Both casts die early.

The lead budget

An ounce per ten feet of depth per mile an hour of current is the drift honest number. The quarter-ounce drawer belongs to a lake at dawn.

What it never does

It never trusts a diameter chart from the same company selling the line, never calls a sinker exact, and never promises the fish. The tables are labeled norms; the river keeps its own ledger.

Reel Math is open source - the engine is small enough to read, and the math is plain enough to argue with.
